See Yourself as a Leader — Honestly

You got the title. Now decide what kind of leader you are building toward. Five sprint actions to set your foundation.

Step 1 · Career Mindset

Action 1 · Score

Complete the Team Lead Scoreboard using evidence from the last three months, not feeling.

Why it works: A self-assessment without evidence is a guess.

Action 2 · Plot

Place yourself on the Career Chessboard: impact in current role vs. readiness for next.

Why it works: You need a starting point before you can plan a route.

Action 3 · Listen

Ask one direct report how they would describe your leadership style. Listen without defending.

Why it works: The gap between intent and perception is where growth lives.

Action 4 · Declare

Write your 18-month ambition: the scope, the role, the skill level you are building toward.

Why it works: A vague goal pulls no one forward.

Action 5 · Prioritize

Name the one leadership habit that would change your team’s output most.

Why it works: One habit mastered beats five half-adopted.

Run the Team — Like a Business

You own the team’s output now. These sprint actions turn management theory into daily operating rhythm.

Step 2 · Master Your Role & Team Performance

Action 1 · Map

Assess each team member’s capability vs. engagement. Decide who needs coaching, stretch, or structure.

Why it works: Leading everyone the same way means leading no one well.

Action 2 · Delegate

Choose one task you are still doing that should belong to someone else. Hand it over with support.

Why it works: Delegation builds people. Hoarding builds dependency.

Action 3 · Confront

Have the conversation you have been avoiding. Prepare the evidence, use the SBI model, deliver it.

Why it works: Feedback delayed becomes a problem compounded.

Action 4 · Rhythm

Set one weekly routine (huddle, 1:1, or quality review) and run it without missing once this sprint.

Why it works: Consistency signals that the team operates with intention.

Action 5 · Diagnose

Identify one process metric that is slipping. Own the root cause before anyone asks you about it.

Why it works: Leaders who find problems early are trusted with bigger scope.

Build Your Team’s Reputation

Your team’s work is invisible until you make it visible. Five sprint actions to build credit for your people and for yourself.

Step 3 · Visibility & Team Reputation

Action 1 · Credit

Name one team member’s contribution publicly — in a meeting, email, or standup.

Why it works: Credit that flows down builds loyalty. Credit that flows up builds reputation.

Action 2 · Elevate

Share one team result with your manager’s manager, framed in business terms.

Why it works: Visibility skips levels when the message is right.

Action 3 · Navigate

Handle one salary or promotion question with honesty and a clear next step.

Why it works: How you handle these moments defines your leadership brand.

Action 4 · Report

Write a team highlight for the next leadership update — one paragraph, business impact.

Why it works: A team that can prove its value gets resourced. One that can’t gets cut.

Action 5 · Rehearse

Prepare a two-minute summary of your team’s value. Be ready for any audience.

Why it works: The question will come. The leaders who have an answer get remembered.

Think Like a Manager — Before You Are One

The gap between Team Lead and Manager is financial literacy, org design, and succession. Close it now.

Step 4 · Next-Level Capabilities

Action 1 · Cost it

Ask your manager for your team’s cost-to-serve number. Understand every line.

Why it works: Managers think in P&L. Team Leads who do the same get promoted.

Action 2 · De-risk

Identify your single point of failure. Start the knowledge transfer this sprint.

Why it works: You cannot move up while the team depends on one person for everything.

Action 3 · Scale

Sketch how your function would work with double the scope. What changes, what breaks.

Why it works: Thinking at function level is the proof you are ready for more.

Action 4 · Read the numbers

Read one P&L or budget document end to end. Note what surprises you.

Why it works: Financial literacy separates team leads from managers more than any other skill.

Action 5 · Succession plan

Draft a succession plan for your own role. Name who takes over, and what they need first.

Why it works: A leader who has a successor is free to move. One who does not is stuck.

Make the Case — or Make Peace

The evidence is built. Now use it: make the promotion ask, or decide your real next move with clarity.

Step 5 · Execute

Action 1 · Compile

Pull your Brag Sheet together with starred wins from every step of this path.

Why it works: A promotion case is built on proof, not hope.

Action 2 · Write

Draft the promotion case: impact delivered, capabilities built, readiness evidence.

Why it works: The document forces clarity. If you struggle to fill it, you know where the gap is.

Action 3 · Advocate

Identify your two strongest internal advocates. Brief them before your ask.

Why it works: Promotions are decided in rooms you are not in. Advocates speak for you there.

Action 4 · Ask

Book the career conversation. Use the Word-for-Word opener from the module.

Why it works: A clear, calm ask is hard to brush off.

Action 5 · Plan B

If the answer is not yet: decide whether to develop further, pivot to projects, or move externally.

Why it works: A plan B turns a no into a timeline — or a redirect into a better fit.
