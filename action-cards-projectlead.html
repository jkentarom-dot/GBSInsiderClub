Think Like a Leader — Not a Doer

The shift from contributor to leader starts with how you scope, assess risk, and see stakeholders. Five sprint actions to build that mindset.

Step 1 · Project Leadership Mindset

Action 1 · Scope it

Write a one-paragraph scope statement for your project. If it takes more, it is too broad.

Why it works: Projects fail on scope more than on execution. Clarity here prevents chaos later.

Action 2 · Risk-bet

Identify the three project risks you would bet your bonus on. Document them before anyone asks.

Why it works: A leader who surfaces risk early gets trusted with bigger projects.

Action 3 · Real metric

Name your project’s real success metric — the one the sponsor actually cares about beyond time and budget.

Why it works: On time and on budget is the minimum. Sponsors care about the business outcome.

Action 4 · Stakeholder map

Map who blocks, who enables, and who decides. Mark the ones you have not spoken to yet.

Why it works: Unengaged stakeholders become surprised stakeholders — and surprised stakeholders block.

Action 5 · Boundaries

Define your charter’s non-negotiable scope boundaries — the items you will protect.

Why it works: Without boundaries, scope grows until the project collapses under its own weight.

Plan the Work — Then Work the Plan

Structure separates a project leader from someone who is busy. Five sprint actions to build real project discipline.

Step 2 · Master the Craft

Action 1 · Break it down

Build a WBS to level 3. If you cannot decompose it, you cannot estimate it.

Why it works: A WBS is the skeleton of the project. Without it, everything is a guess.

Action 2 · Choose method

Pick CI, Waterfall, or Agile for your project. Write a one-sentence rationale.

Why it works: The right method depends on the project, not on preference. Justify the choice.

Action 3 · Root cause

Run one structured problem-solving exercise (A3 or 5 Whys) on a real project issue.

Why it works: Structured method impresses more than “we fixed it.” It also works better.

Action 4 · Govern it

Set up your RAID log and governance cadence: who reviews what, when, and with what authority.

Why it works: Governance prevents derailment. Projects that skip it spend twice the effort recovering.

Action 5 · Dependencies

Identify the three longest dependencies in your plan and confirm who owns each one.

Why it works: An unowned dependency is a risk in disguise.

Manage the People — They Are Harder Than the Plan

Stakeholder management is where good projects succeed and great plans fail. Five sprint actions to stay ahead.

Step 3 · Stakeholders & Communication

Action 1 · Sponsor check

Have a direct conversation with your sponsor. Ask what failure looks like to them.

Why it works: A sponsor’s definition of failure is your real constraint. Learn it early.

Action 2 · Steerco ready

Prepare and deliver one steering committee update — even if it is a dry run.

Why it works: Presenting under governance pressure is a skill that only practice builds.

Action 3 · Difficult message

Write one stakeholder message that delivers uncomfortable news: scope change, delay, or resource ask.

Why it works: Difficult messages delivered well build credibility. Avoided ones build distrust.

Action 4 · Influence map

Map your stakeholders from resistant to supportive. Plan one move to shift someone.

Why it works: Influence is built deliberately. Hoping people come around is a plan for stalling.

Action 5 · Champion

Identify and brief your project champion — the person who defends it when you are not in the room.

Why it works: Every project needs someone fighting for it in the meetings you are not invited to.

Land the Change — Then Prove It Worked

Delivery without adoption is a failed project. Five sprint actions to land the change and prove the value.

Step 4 · Land It & Prove Value

Action 1 · Change comms

Build a change communication plan: who hears what, when, from whom.

Why it works: Surprise is the enemy of adoption. A comms plan prevents the “no one told us” problem.

Action 2 · Hard savings

Quantify one project outcome in hard savings terms. Get Finance to validate.

Why it works: Soft savings are a conversation. Hard savings are a fact. Learn the difference.

Action 3 · Hypercare

Design your hypercare model: who covers what, for how long, with what escalation path.

Why it works: A planned hypercare period turns go-live panic into controlled stabilization.

Action 4 · Retro

Run a retrospective: what went well, what broke, what you would do differently.

Why it works: A project leader who reflects publicly earns the next assignment.

Action 5 · Benefits report

Draft a benefits realization report: actual results vs. charter commitments.

Why it works: The report is your proof of value. Without it, the project is a cost, not an investment.

Build the Portfolio — Then Make the Move

Your project history is career currency. Five sprint actions to turn it into your next role.

Step 5 · Execute Your Career Move

Action 1 · Portfolio

Compile your project portfolio: scope, role, results, and lessons for each project led.

Why it works: A portfolio is proof of pattern, not just proof of one win.

Action 2 · Narrative

Write the story: what kind of project leader you are and what you bring.

Why it works: Facts open doors. A narrative gets you through them.

Action 3 · Target

Name your next target: bigger project, PM role, or management track.

Why it works: A vague ambition is easy to ignore. A specific ask gets a specific answer.

Action 4 · Advocate

Brief your advocates with your portfolio before the career conversation.

Why it works: Advocates who have your evidence can make your case when you are not there.

Action 5 · Ask

Make the ask: promotion, new assignment, or external move.

Why it works: The work is done. Now own the conversation.
