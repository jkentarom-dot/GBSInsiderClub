Land on the Project — Without Losing Your BAU

You just got pulled onto a project. First priority: understand the rules and protect your day job.

Week 1 · Understand the Project

Action 1 · Read

Read the project charter. Note what you are actually expected to deliver.

Why it works: Assumptions about your role get corrected in the first week or never.

Action 2 · Protect

Have the capacity conversation: agree with your TL what BAU work gets adjusted.

Why it works: Without a signed agreement, project work eats BAU — and you get blamed for both.

Action 3 · Map

Draw the project team: who is the PM, your counterpart, and who makes decisions.

Why it works: Knowing who decides is more valuable than knowing who does.

Action 4 · Clarify

Ask what you can approve vs. what you must escalate. Get the answer in writing.

Why it works: Sign-off authority confusion causes the biggest project delays.

Action 5 · Track

Set up your dual tracker: BAU commitments alongside project deliverables.

Why it works: Two jobs need one view. Separate trackers create separate failures.

Transfer Knowledge Like a Professional

KT is your moment. The quality of what you document now determines the pain level after go-live.

Week 2 · Knowledge Transfer

Action 1 · Walk the model

Learn the 4-Phase KT approach: Prepare, Execute, Validate, Sign-off.

Why it works: A framework prevents you from missing the step that causes post-go-live chaos.

Action 2 · Document

Write one critical process so that a stranger could run it tomorrow.

Why it works: If it depends on memory, it depends on a person who might leave.

Action 3 · Uncover

Identify three things only you know. Start writing them down.

Why it works: Undocumented knowledge is the project’s biggest hidden risk.

Action 4 · Risk-log

Create a risk log for KT gaps: what is undocumented and who holds it.

Why it works: Naming the gap is the first step toward closing it.

Action 5 · Test it

Have someone execute the process using only your documentation.

Why it works: Documentation that hasn’t been tested is documentation that doesn’t work.

Test It Properly — Not Just Click Through

UAT is where you protect the business. Thorough testing now saves months of firefighting later.

Week 3 · Testing

Action 1 · Scenario-first

Write test cases tied to business scenarios, not system steps.

Why it works: Systems pass tech tests and fail real work. Business scenarios catch what tech misses.

Action 2 · Edge cases

Run one end-to-end test that includes the exceptions and edge cases.

Why it works: The happy path always works. Edge cases are where go-lives break.

Action 3 · Log properly

Document every defect with clear steps to reproduce and business impact.

Why it works: A well-logged defect gets fixed. A vague one gets deprioritized.

Action 4 · Escalate

Raise one blocker through the proper channel — RAID log, not hallway chat.

Why it works: Escalation through structure gets action. Hallway complaints get sympathy.

Action 5 · Exit criteria

Review what “pass” actually means before you sign off.

Why it works: Signing off without clear criteria means owning the outcome without controlling the standard.

Raise Issues Without Looking Negative

Project communication has its own rules. Learn them and your voice carries weight, not friction.

Week 4 · Communication

Action 1 · RAID it

Write your first RAID log entry: one Risk or Issue with impact and mitigation.

Why it works: Structured risk-raising is leadership. Unstructured complaining is noise.

Action 2 · Status update

Submit one project status update using the standard template.

Why it works: Consistent reporting builds your reputation as someone the PM can rely on.

Action 3 · Ask publicly

Ask one clarifying question about scope in a project meeting.

Why it works: Asking early prevents rework. The question you hold back is usually the one everyone has.

Action 4 · Flag

Identify one dependency that could derail your area’s timeline.

Why it works: Catching a dependency early is proactive. Catching it late is a crisis.

Action 5 · Bridge

Send one proactive update to your BAU manager about project progress.

Why it works: Keeping your BAU stakeholder informed prevents the “where were you?” conversation.

Land It Cleanly — Then Survive Hypercare

Go-live is a team effort. Your preparation determines whether your area lands smoothly or scrambles.

Week 5 · Go-Live

Action 1 · Ready check

Complete your area’s go-live readiness checklist.

Why it works: Readiness is binary: ready or not. Guessing gets people hurt.

Action 2 · Anticipate

Identify the three most likely post-go-live issues and prepare responses.

Why it works: The issues you planned for are the ones that don’t become crises.

Action 3 · Hypercare model

Set up your hypercare tracking: who escalates what, to whom, how fast.

Why it works: Hypercare without a model is just overtime with extra stress.

Action 4 · Brief the team

Tell your BAU team what changes and what support they will need.

Why it works: Surprise is the enemy of adoption. Briefed teams adapt faster.

Action 5 · Exit plan

Define your personal exit criteria: when is your project involvement done.

Why it works: Without exit criteria, project work expands until someone burns out.

Convert the Project Into a Career Move

The project is winding down. Make sure the experience counts for your career — on purpose.

Week 6 · Career Leverage

Action 1 · Bank it

Update your Brag Sheet with project contributions and measurable results.

Why it works: Experience without evidence is just a memory. Evidence is a career asset.

Action 2 · Reference

Ask the PM for written feedback or a project reference.

Why it works: A reference from a PM carries weight with hiring managers and leadership.

Action 3 · Name it

List the skills this project built that your BAU role did not.

Why it works: New skills are your return on investment from the project workload.

Action 4 · Decide

Choose your next move: back to BAU with new skills, or pursue project work.

Why it works: A deliberate choice beats a default drift back to the old routine.

Action 5 · Position it

Update your LinkedIn and prepare the project narrative for your next review.

Why it works: The right framing turns six weeks of extra work into a career step.
