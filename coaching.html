One to one — Personal Coaching

A coach sees what a page cannot.

The site gives you the models, the vocabulary, and the moves. A coach looks at your actual situation — your manager, your team, your market, your timing — and tells you what a page never can: whether this is the right move, the right moment, and the right version of you to make it.

Who it is for

You have the ambition. What you lack is clarity.

You are not sure what to focus on first, or you suspect there are gaps you cannot name yet. You need someone to look at the whole picture — your skills, your job market, what your company actually needs right now — and help you see where the real opening is.

A coach saves time. Self-directed learning works, but it takes longer because you do not know what you do not know. If timing matters — a promotion round in three months, a reorganization, a role you want before someone else gets it — a coach compresses the learning into the window that counts.

Who it is not for

If a page can solve it, use the page. Most questions about frameworks, vocabulary, and how a process works belong in the free tier or the $29 playbooks — not a coaching call.

A faster path is not the same as a shorter one. The skills you build are what sustain the role and let you grow beyond it. Skipping that part puts you in a position where you feel unprepared and under stress — and coaching will not fix that.

Progressing steadily, with the right focus at the right time, puts you in a stronger position than arriving early and scrambling to keep up.

The process

How it works

One hour per session, video call, one to one. It starts with a free intro call where we scope things together — your need, your ambition, and your budget.

The assessment

We start with a real read of where you stand today. Your ambitions first — what do you actually want, and how sure are you?

Then your situation. What does your current role demand, what does your company need, and what does the external market look like for someone with your profile? This is the same logic as a SWOT for a company, applied to your career. Supply, demand, and the gap between what you offer today and what you could offer in six months.

The work between sessions

Every session ends with something practical to do before the next one — not reading, not reflecting, doing.

The real value is not in the hour we talk. Muscles are not built in the gym. They grow afterwards, through rest and the right nutrition, after they received the right stimulus. The coaching session is the stimulus. The growth happens at your desk, in your next meeting, in the conversation you were avoiding.

What changes

Two real situations from coaching sessions.

The project lead who almost said no

A process analyst was offered a project lead role and hesitated. The skills were there. What was missing was readiness — for the cognitive load, the hours, the sacrifice that comes with leading a project that matters.

The hesitation was real, but it was about to be read as something else: lack of confidence, lack of willingness. The people offering the role do not wait long to move on.

Coaching put the challenge and the benefits side by side, honestly. The analyst took the role with open eyes instead of losing it to silence.

The team lead who needed a different game

A team lead wanted the next level. Salary, title, scope. The technical skills were strong. What was missing was the political game.

At the next level up, your stakeholder population expands — not by a little, but exponentially. What you say, how you say it and who hears it all change. The gap was not competence. It was mindset: learning to stop saying everything you think, learning to read how the same sentence lands with five different audiences.

That shift does not come from a framework on a page. It comes from someone telling you, in your specific context, which conversation to have differently on Monday.

What a page cannot do

Every model on this site works. The career map is real, the playbooks are tested, the Fast Wins produce results in five days. But none of them know your manager. None of them can hear the thing you are not saying. None of them can tell you whether the problem is the company or you — and that distinction matters more than any framework.

The social and psychological side of a career move is sometimes the whole game. A page can explain the model. A coach can tell you why you are not using it.
