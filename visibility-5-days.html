How do I get more visibility?

Five working days. About twenty minutes each. You already know your job — this is about being seen doing it.

5 days · 20 min per day · Progress saves on this device
Klaudia Year 3 · Krakow

She knows the answer.
She waits for the right moment.
The moment passes.

Someone else says a worse version of it.
Everyone nods.
Her manager writes it down — under his name.

The trap

Being right in your head is invisible. Nobody promotes what they cannot see.

1

Copy what works

Monday · 20 min

Before you change anything, study the people who already have what you want.

  • Pick two or three people who are visible and respected. Not the loudest ones. The ones whose name comes up when they are not in the room.
  • Watch them for one week. Write down how they speak — volume, pace, and when they stay quiet.
  • Note what they attach themselves to. Which topics? Which meetings? Whose messages do they answer fast?
  • Note how they show up. How they dress. How they sit. Whether they wait to be asked.
  • Pick the two habits closest to who you already are. Ignore the rest.
Do not skip this

You are borrowing a pattern, not a personality. Copy the two habits that fit you. The rest will look fake and everyone will feel it.

2

Find something worth saying

Tuesday · 20 min

Speaking up only works when what you say is worth the air. Build the substance first.

  • Every good contribution takes one of two shapes. A question that moves the discussion, or information that saves someone work. Pick your shape.
  • Go get the substance. Read the last three updates from a neighboring team. You will find a gap nobody has named yet.
  • Ask a senior colleague: "What do you actually think about X?" People tell you more than you expect. Listen for what they are worried about.
  • Use AI to fill your knowledge holes. Ask it to explain the thing you pretend to understand.
  • Write three specific things you could say next week that nobody else in the room could say.
Cut these

"Just to add to that." "I agree with what was said." "Good point." Empty sentences cost you more than silence.

3

Break your own idea

Wednesday · 20 min

Anything you say in front of leadership gets one follow-up question. Survive it here first, in private.

  • Take your three things to AI. Tell it: "Be a skeptical director. Challenge this. What do you ask me next?"
  • Answer the follow-up. If you cannot, you do not have a point yet — you have an opinion. Go back and get the number, the example, or the source.
  • Ask it to argue the opposite. Find out what the strongest objection sounds like before someone in the room says it.
  • Keep the one item that survives. Cut the other two, or park them.
  • Say your surviving point out loud. If it takes more than twenty seconds, it is not ready.
Why this day matters

This is where your idea stops being a thought and becomes a position. A position holds up when questioned. That is the whole difference.

Your surviving point, in twenty seconds
4

Say it in a small room

Thursday · 20 min

First run goes somewhere cheap. Team meeting. One-to-one. A reply that adds something instead of just agreeing.

  • Pick one low-stakes room. Today. Not next week.
  • Say your point once, clearly. Then stop talking. Do not soften it, do not repeat it, do not apologize for it.
  • Watch what happens in the three seconds after. Who looks up? Who writes it down? Who asks you something?
  • Kill the hedges. "I might be wrong, but" and "sorry, quick question" delete your point before you make it.
  • Write down what actually happened. One line.
If your voice shakes

Slow down instead of speeding up. Nervous people rush. Senior people pause. Try one deliberate pause before your first sentence.

5

Say it where it counts

Friday · 20 min

Now put it in front of people above your line. This is the day the week pays for itself.

  • Pick one room with seniority in it. A townhall question. A project update. A written summary you send one level up.
  • Lead with the answer, not the build-up. Senior people decide in the first sentence whether to keep listening.
  • If speaking is not an option this week, write it. A four-line message to the right person does the same job.
  • Afterwards, write two lines. What landed. What you would drop.
  • Put a reminder in your calendar. Run this again next month, starting at day 1. Visibility is a habit, not an event.
The real test

You will know it worked when someone repeats your point back to you weeks later and thinks it was theirs. Let them. Your name is already attached to it in the room that mattered.
