How the calculator works

Method, what is included and what is not
  1. Cost today. For each country and role: jobs times average pay, plus bonus, employer charges, office and IT. It grows each year with wages in those countries. The retained team and the offices that are kept stay; the rest goes away step by step as work moves.
  2. Run cost of the new center. Jobs that do the work times the cost per job (pay, bonus, employer charges, language bonus, office and IT), plus the fixed site running cost, plus the turnover difference to today. After the move you need fewer people each year (efficiency). Cost grows with wages at the site: a site that is 5 percent cheaper today but grows 2 to 3 points faster per year is more expensive by year 4.
  3. One-time cost, in three blocks. A, people leaving: severance, outplacement and retention bonuses are paid when each job leaves; works council and legal advice during preparation. B, site: site set-up and office fit-out during preparation, IT equipment when people are hired. A fit-out lasts about 10 years, so the 5-year view gives back the value of the later years. C, transition: new people paid before they take over the work (double cost), extra people for the first 12 months after each team goes live, recruiting and travel when people are hired, and the project team from decision to the last go-live.
  4. One-time income. Sale of offices and cash freed come in when the move is done.
  5. Net effect. Cost today minus what is still paid at the old locations, minus run cost of the new center, plus other savings, minus one-time cost. Month by month for 60 months.
  6. Results. Payback is the first month the running total is above zero. NPV is the 5-year total with later money counted less. IRR is the yearly return on the money you put in. This is a cash view: severance is booked as a provision when the move is announced, and fit-out is capex written off over its life. Your P&L shows these at other times than the cash does.

Not included: tax and incentives, transfer pricing between your companies, exchange rates, several new sites in one option, and different speeds for different teams. These usually move the result by 10 to 20 percent.

Data confidence. Every country and site has a confidence score: green 70 percent and above (recent salary guides), amber 50 to 69 percent (partly estimated), red below 50 percent (mostly estimates). The result cards show the score for the site and for your countries. Where the score is low, replace the averages with your own numbers in the detail settings.

How exact is it? We checked it against a detailed month-by-month model with the same cost levels. The difference was about 4 percent of the 5-year cost, and payback differed by 0 to 5 months. Your real cost per job matters much more than that difference. Enter your roles per country, or your own payroll cost per FTE.
