How the calculator works

  1. Cost if nothing changes. For each country and role: number of jobs times average pay, plus bonus, employer charges, office and IT. It grows each year with wages in those countries.
  2. Old locations. During the move, work leaves in equal monthly steps. You pay the old locations for the work that has not moved yet.
  3. New center. New people start before they take over the work (the double-cost months). In the first year you carry extra people. After that you need fewer people each year. Cost grows with wages in the new country.
  4. One-time cost. Severance is paid when each job leaves. All other one-time cost is spread over preparation and move.
  5. Net effect. Line 1 minus lines 2, 3 and 4, month by month for 60 months.
  6. Results. Payback is the first month the running total is above zero. NPV is the 5-year total with later money counted less. IRR is the yearly return on the money you put in.

Not included: tax, grants and subsidies, exchange rates, work that stays behind, staff turnover cost, several new sites in one option, and different speeds for different teams or countries. These usually move the result by 10 to 20 percent.

How exact is it? We checked it against a detailed month-by-month model with the same cost levels. The difference was about 4 percent of the 5-year cost, and payback differed by 0 to 5 months. Your real cost per job matters much more than that difference. Enter your roles per country, or your own payroll cost per FTE.
