Transition

Transition strategy, knowledge transfer execution, stabilization, and the operational resilience that determines whether a GBS migration succeeds or fails.

Transition is where GBS theory meets operational reality. Moving processes from one location, team, or model to another is the highest-stakes activity in GBS — it affects people, performance, and client confidence simultaneously. This pillar covers the methodology and discipline that separate clean transitions from chaotic ones.

BLIND SPOT

The smartest transition plans invest in both sides — the receiving hub and the retained organization. Most playbooks focus heavily on the team taking over: training, resources, investment, ramp-up. That makes sense, but the retained team needs just as much clarity. They need to know how the new process works, who to contact at the hub, what the handoff points look like, and what to expect post-go-live. When both sides are prepared, transitions land cleaner and stabilize faster. If you're involved in a transition — on either side — make sure the retained organization has a clear playbook too. It's one of the highest-impact things you can advocate for, and it's often the difference between a smooth landing and months of firefighting.

10.1 Transition Strategy

Methodologies: Lift and Shift vs. Transform and Transfer
Project Mgr
+

Lift and Shift moves a process as-is to the GBS centre and optimises later. Transform and Transfer redesigns the target state before migration. Most successful GBS programmes use a hybrid: tactical lift and shift for early waves to build credibility, with transform and transfer for subsequent waves. The choice shapes your timeline, risk profile, and whether you end up running a cost centre or a transformation engine.

Due Diligence and Scope Validation
Project Mgr
+

Due diligence is the process of verifying what you are actually transitioning — volumes, complexity, systems, exceptions, dependencies, and people. The gap between what the business case assumed and what due diligence reveals is where transition timelines and budgets die. Never skip due diligence. The cost of discovering surprises mid-transition is 10x the cost of finding them upfront.

Transition Governance and Gate Reviews
Project Mgr
+

Gate reviews are formal checkpoints where a steering committee decides whether a transition is ready to proceed to the next phase. Each gate has entry criteria (what must be true) and exit criteria (what must be complete). Passing a gate without meeting criteria — which happens under time pressure — is the single most common cause of transition failures.

10.2 KT Execution

The 4-Phase KT Model (Shadow, Reverse Shadow, Handover, Sign-off)
Team Lead
+

Phase 1: the receiving team shadows the sending team (watch and learn). Phase 2: reverse shadow — the receiving team executes while the sending team watches (do and correct). Phase 3: handover — the receiving team operates independently with support available. Phase 4: sign-off — formal acceptance that knowledge transfer is complete. Skipping phases or compressing timelines is how you get a team that can execute the happy path but panics at the first exception.

Managing Cultural Resistance During Transition
Team Lead
+

The sending team often feels their jobs are being taken. The receiving team feels overwhelmed by unfamiliar processes. Both are right. Managing cultural resistance during transition requires acknowledging what people are losing, providing clarity on what comes next, and creating space for the emotional reality of change alongside the operational plan.

Service Acceptance Criteria (SAC)
Project Mgr
+

SAC defines the objective conditions under which the receiving team formally accepts ownership of a transitioned process. It typically includes accuracy rates, processing volumes, exception handling capability, and documentation completeness. Without SAC, the moment of handover becomes a negotiation. With SAC, it is a measurement.

10.3 Stabilization

Hypercare Management and Exit Criteria
Team Lead
+

Hypercare is the intensive support period immediately after go-live — typically 4-12 weeks. During hypercare, the sending team remains available, escalation paths are shortened, and performance is monitored daily. Exit criteria define when hypercare ends and normal operations begin. Exiting hypercare too early creates risk. Staying in hypercare too long creates dependency.

Baselining Performance Post-Go-Live
Pro
+

Baselining means establishing the new team performance benchmark after transition — processing times, error rates, SLA compliance, and stakeholder satisfaction. The baseline is not compared to the pre-transition state (which had years of optimization); it is the starting point for the new team continuous improvement journey. Setting the right baseline prevents unfair comparisons and unrealistic expectations.

Operational Resilience: Recovery from System Outages
Project Mgr
+

Operational resilience is the ability to maintain service delivery when things go wrong — system outages, staff shortages, data center failures. In GBS, resilience planning includes manual fallback procedures, cross-trained staff, geographic redundancy, and tested recovery playbooks. The organizations that recover fastest are the ones that planned for failure before it happened.
